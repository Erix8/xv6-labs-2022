Q: Why are there missing keys with 2 threads, but not with 1 thread?
   Identify a sequence of events with 2 threads that can lead to a key
   being missing.

A: put() is not atomic. Two threads may concurrently insert into the same
   hash bucket, and one writer may overwrite the other's update.

   Suppose T1 inserts key k1 and T2 inserts key k2, where both keys hash
   to the same (initially empty) bucket i:

   1. T1 evaluates insert(k1, v1, &table[i], NULL), so it creates entry
      e1 with e1->next = NULL.
   2. T2 evaluates insert(k2, v2, &table[i], NULL), so it creates entry
      e2 with e2->next = NULL.  Step 2 may interleave with step 1.
   3. T1 executes *p = e1, i.e. table[i] = e1.
   4. T2 executes *p = e2, i.e. table[i] = e2, overwriting e1.

   Now table[i] points only to e2; e1 (and therefore key k1) is lost, so
   a later get(k1) finds nothing and reports the key as missing.

   With 1 thread the inserts are serialized: the second insert reads the
   first entry as table[i] and chains onto it (e2->next = e1), so no key
   is lost.  With 2 threads the read of table[i] by one thread and the
   write back by the same thread are not atomic as a whole, producing the
   lost update above.
